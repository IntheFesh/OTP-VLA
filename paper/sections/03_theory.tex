\section{Theory: Architecture Conditions and the Demonstration-Level Bound}
\label{sec:theory}

This section formalizes the conditions on policy architecture and demonstration data that
organize the empirical findings of \S\ref{sec:results}. \S\ref{sec:theory:setup}
introduces notation and the C2 and C3 conditions on policy architecture.
\S\ref{sec:theory:bound} (Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}) states the architecture-agnostic upper
bound on policy-level language conditioning by demonstration-level language conditioning.

\subsection{Setup and Architectural Conditions}
\label{sec:theory:setup}

\subsubsection{Notation}
\label{sec:theory:notation}

We consider a policy $\pi: \actO \times \actL \to \Delta(\actA)$ mapping
observation-instruction pairs to distributions over robot actions, trained on
demonstration data $\dataD(o, \ell, a)$ over observation space $\actO$, instruction space
$\actL$, and action space $\actA$. We decompose the policy as
\begin{equation}
\pi(a \mid o, \ell) \;=\; \phitheta(a \mid z, w(o)),
\label{eq:policy_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $w(o)$ denotes observation-derived features (visual encodings, proprioception,
geometry, affordance maps; see \S\ref{sec:architecture} for instantiation), and $z =
\hpsi(o, \ell)$ is an instruction-aware latent produced by an upstream module (the
\emph{head} in our architecture). The decoder $\phitheta$ is a stochastic mapping that
produces actions conditional on the latent $z$ and observation features $w(o)$.

This decomposition is sufficiently general to cover most VLA architectures considered in
\S\ref{sec:related:vla}: in OpenVLA~\cite{kim2024openvla} and RT-2~\cite{brohan2023rt2},
$z$ corresponds to the LLM hidden state at the last text token, $w(o)$ is implicit in
visual tokenization, and $\phitheta$ is a softmax over discretized action tokens; in
OFT~\cite{kim2025openvla_oft}, $z$ is the same LLM hidden state and $\phitheta$ is a
deterministic regression head; in $\pi_0$~\cite{black2024pi0}, $\phitheta$ is a
flow-matching head; in OTP-Soft (this paper, \S\ref{sec:architecture}), $z$ is an
object-pose trajectory and $\phitheta$ is a Shortcut Flow Matching decoder.

We use $a \sim \dataD(\cdot \mid o, \ell)$ for action samples from the demonstration
distribution conditional on $(o, \ell)$, and $\Astar \sim \pistar(\cdot \mid o, \ell)$ for
action samples from a policy at the same conditioning.

\subsubsection{Condition C2: Task-Discriminative Latent}
\label{sec:theory:c2}

The first condition concerns whether the latent $z$ carries task-discriminative
information beyond what is determined by the observation alone.

\begin{condition}[Task-discriminative latent]
\label{cond:c2}
Let $\tau(\ell)$ denote the instruction equivalence class of $\ell$ under semantic
paraphrase --- that is, $\tau(\ell) = \tau(\ell')$ if and only if $\ell$ and $\ell'$ are
paraphrases referring to the same intended task. We require
\begin{equation}
I(z; \tau \mid o) \;>\; 0.
\label{eq:c2}
\end{equation}
\end{condition}

In words: the latent $z$ must carry information about the task identity (instruction
equivalence class) beyond what the observation $o$ already provides. C2 is a necessary
condition for the policy to behave differently across paraphrased instructions referring
to different tasks while sharing the same observation context.

The use of $\tau$ rather than the raw instruction $\ell$ is important. Two paraphrased
instructions referring to the same task (``pick up the black bowl'' and ``grasp the dark
bowl'') should produce the same conditioning on the policy, even though the language
embeddings of $\ell$ and $\ell'$ differ in surface form. Requiring $I(z; \tau \mid o) > 0$
targets the \emph{semantic} content of language, not the surface form. An earlier
formulation requiring only $H(z \mid o) > 0$ is insufficient: it is satisfied by injecting
any task-independent noise into $z$, which carries no information about the intended task.

C2 is a property of the policy under evaluation, not of the supervision data. In
\S\ref{sec:results:diagnostic} (C1 diagnostic) we measure a related quantity --- the Mantel
correlation $r(z, \ell)$ on policy latents --- as an empirical proxy for whether C2 holds
substantively after training.

\subsubsection{Condition C3: Decoder Language-Agnosticism}
\label{sec:theory:c3}

The second condition concerns whether the decoder $\phitheta$ receives the instruction
$\ell$ directly as input, separately from the routing through $z$.

\begin{condition}[Decoder language-agnosticism]
\label{cond:c3}
The decoder $\phitheta$ does not receive $\ell$ as an input:
\begin{equation}
\ell \;\notin\; \mathrm{inputs}(\phitheta).
\label{eq:c3}
\end{equation}
\end{condition}

All language information that reaches the decoder must flow through the latent $z$.
Equivalently, the decoder's action output $\phitheta(a \mid z, w(o))$ is conditionally
independent of $\ell$ given $(z, w(o))$.

C3 is an \emph{architectural} condition rather than an empirical one. In OTP-Soft
(\S\ref{sec:architecture}), C3 is enforced by construction: the decoder's \texttt{forward()}
signature accepts only $(z, w(o))$ inputs, and a \texttt{\_check\_forbidden} guard raises
\texttt{AssertionError} at both module construction and every forward pass if any
forbidden name fragment related to $\ell$ appears among parameter names, submodule names,
or runtime locals.

The motivation for C3 is structural clarity rather than performance. By routing all
language information through a single bottleneck ($z$), the architecture exposes a clean
diagnostic target: measuring $I(z; \ell \mid o)$ (or its empirical proxy $r(z, \ell)$ in
\S\ref{sec:results:diagnostic}) directly assesses whether the upstream head $\hpsi$ has
preserved language information for the decoder to use. Without C3, the decoder could
potentially route language information through a second, unobserved pathway, conflating
the diagnostic target.

C3 is \emph{not} a sufficient condition for the policy to use language at the action
level. As we show in \S\ref{sec:theory:bound} (Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}) and
\S\ref{sec:results:cfm_collapse} (empirical instantiation), C3 combined with
demonstration-supervised training does not guarantee policy-level language conditioning:
the bound on $I(\Astar; \ell \mid o)$ depends on the demonstration data's language-action
correlation structure, not on the architectural enforcement of C3.

\subsubsection{Relationship Between C2 and C3}
\label{sec:theory:c2_c3_relation}

C2 and C3 address different layers of the policy. C2 is a requirement on the latent $z$
--- it must encode task-discriminative information. C3 is a requirement on the decoder
$\phitheta$ --- it must not bypass $z$ by reading $\ell$ directly. Together, they specify
a clean architectural decomposition where:
\begin{itemize}
\item The head $\hpsi$ is the only module that consumes $\ell$.
\item The decoder $\phitheta$ depends on $\ell$ only indirectly through $z$.
\item The C2 condition's diagnostic ($I(z; \tau \mid o)$) and the policy's empirical
      language conditioning ($I(\Astar; \ell \mid o)$) can be measured independently.
\end{itemize}

Under this decomposition, Theorem~\ref{thm:bound} (\S\ref{sec:theory:bound}) provides the
bound on $I(\Astar; \ell \mid o)$ in terms of demonstration-level signal. The
\S\ref{sec:results:diagnostic} measurements (Gate~1 on the backbone state, M3 on
demonstration trajectories, C1 on $z$) then trace how language information propagates from
the backbone, through the supervision data, into the trained policy's latent.

\subsection{Theorem~3': The Demonstration-Level Upper Bound}
\label{sec:theory:bound}

\begin{definition}[$\eps$-language-orthogonality of demonstration data]
\label{def:eps_lang_orthog}
The demonstration distribution $\dataD$ is $\eps$-language-orthogonal if
\begin{equation}
I_{\dataD}(a; \ell \mid o) \;\le\; \eps.
\label{eq:eps_orthog}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Demonstration-level bound on policy language conditioning]
\label{thm:bound}
Let $\pistar$ be a policy that minimizes a distribution-matching loss with population
minimizer satisfying $\pistar(\cdot \mid o, \ell) = \dataD(\cdot \mid o, \ell)$
almost-surely. Let $\Astar \sim \pistar(\cdot \mid o, \ell)$. Then
\begin{equation}
I(\Astar; \ell \mid o) \;=\; I_{\dataD}(a; \ell \mid o).
\label{eq:thm_bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\dataD$ is $\eps$-language-orthogonal (Def.~\ref{def:eps_lang_orthog}),
\begin{equation}
I(\Astar; \ell \mid o) \;\le\; \eps.
\label{eq:thm_bound_eps}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
By assumption, $\pistar(\cdot \mid o, \ell) = \dataD(\cdot \mid o, \ell)$ a.s. Hence the
sampled-action distribution under $\pistar$ matches the demonstration-action distribution
conditional on $(o, \ell)$, so the conditional mutual information of the sampled action
with $\ell$ given $o$ equals that of the demonstration action: $I(\Astar; \ell \mid o) =
I_{\dataD}(a; \ell \mid o)$. The bound (\ref{eq:thm_bound_eps}) follows from
Definition~\ref{def:eps_lang_orthog}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Approximate-form bound for empirical policies]
\label{rem:approx_bound}
For empirical policies $\hat\pi$ trained on finite samples ($n \approx 1.1 \times 10^{5}$
demonstration transitions on LIBERO-Spatial), exact recovery $\hat\pi = \dataD$ does not
hold; rather, $\hat\pi \to \dataD$ in distribution as $n \to \infty$. Under standard
regularity conditions on the loss and architecture, the data-processing inequality gives
the approximate-form bound
\begin{equation}
I(\hat A; \ell \mid o) \;\le\; \eps \;+\; o(1),
\label{eq:approx_bound}
\end{equation}
where $\hat A \sim \hat\pi(\cdot \mid o, \ell)$ and the $o(1)$ term vanishes as $n \to
\infty$. Sample-complexity rates are outside the present scope.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Architecture-agnosticism]
\label{rem:arch_agnostic}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bound} applies uniformly to any loss family whose population minimizer
recovers $\dataD(\cdot \mid o, \ell)$. This includes $L_1$ and $L_2$ regression on
continuous actions, cross-entropy on discretized actions, Conditional Flow
Matching~\cite{lipman2023flow_matching}, Shortcut Flow Matching~\cite{frans2024shortcut},
and denoising score matching for diffusion models~\cite{chi2023diffusion}. Changing the
decoder family does not change the bound; it changes the empirical character of policy
behavior within the bound (see \S\ref{sec:results:cfm_collapse} for the CFM-specific
characterization).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Bypass requires interventions outside demonstration-supervised loss]
\label{rem:bypass}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bound} applies only to losses whose population minimizer matches the
demonstration distribution. Bypassing the bound requires interventions outside this loss
family --- for instance, auxiliary language objectives (contrastive
language--observation alignment, language-prediction heads), inference-time conditioning
on backbone-derived signals not present in $\dataD$, or supervision data augmented with
language-conditional reward signals. We discuss such directions only as future work
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{remark}
